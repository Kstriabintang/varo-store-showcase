One system for a village store in Riau.

Varo Store sells phone credit, data, electricity tokens, e-wallet top-ups, game vouchers and bill payments in Kempas, Indragiri Hilir, Riau: at the counter on a tablet kasir, and online from the customer's phone. The owner runs both from one panel and a Telegram bot.

What it is

Two ways to sell, one place to see the money

Customers either walk in or order from their phone. Every order, every rupiah in the cash drawer and every item on the shelf ends up in the same panel.

At the store

A tablet kasir sells digital products, goods and bills in one cart, takes cash, the store's QRIS or a bank transfer, and prints a 58 or 80 mm receipt. Every sale lands in the open shift.

Online

Customers pick a product, pay the store's QRIS with a unique amount, and the order is sent to the supplier as soon as staff confirm the payment. The order page updates itself.

For the owner

A web panel and a Telegram bot: what needs action, today's and this month's numbers, stock, payment matching against the bank statement, and an audit trail nobody can quietly edit.

Online shop

Every category in one look

Credit, data, electricity tokens, e-wallets, game vouchers and bills, in the store's plum and gold.

  • Operator detected from the number as the customer types it.
  • Game account fields per game: User ID and Zone ID for Mobile Legends, a player ID for others.
  • A receipt preview before paying, so the customer sees exactly what they buy.
  • Official artwork and logos for 79 games, operators, e-wallets and billers.
Payment and tracking

Pay by QRIS, watch the order finish

The store uses its own QRIS. Each order asks for a slightly unique amount, so staff can match every payment in the bank statement.

  • 30-minute countdown and step-by-step instructions next to the code.
  • Live order page: created, paid, processing, done, with the supplier's reference number.
  • Track an order by the code on the receipt, or from the list of orders placed on this device.
  • Masked numbers on every public page and receipt.
Bills

Check first, pay when it is right

Electricity, 30 water utilities, health and employment insurance, internet, postpaid mobile, cable TV, gas and instalments.

  • Amount, fee and total shown before anything is paid.
  • The check is bound to that product and customer number, for that day only.
  • Paid means paid: a bill only counts once the supplier returns its serial number.

Built for a 1024 × 768 tablet. One cart for digital products, goods and bills; cash, the store's QRIS or bank transfer; round prices in whole thousands that never go below the online price.

Owner panel

What needs action comes first

Payments waiting, refunds owed, unmatched payments and low stock sit at the top; the numbers come after. Staff see only the menus and figures their permissions allow.

Stock and audit

Goods on the shelf, and a trail nobody can rewrite

Physical goods have barcodes, low-stock thresholds, deliveries, stock counts and write-offs. Every staff action is stored as a hash chain that can be verified with one button.

Telegram bot and reports

The business, from the owner's phone

A management bot with a fixed keyboard reads the same numbers as the panel. It also pushes what matters: orders and refunds, low stock, low supplier balance, staff logins, a daily summary at 21:00 and a monthly report on the 1st.

Reports in the panel, as CSV and as PDF: the monthly report, an internal price list, and a wall price list for the store in A4 or as an A3 poster. Every transaction is also mirrored to Google Sheets, one tab per business line, with numbers masked and no customer contact data.

Engineering highlights

Money moves once, and it is written down

The interesting work in a small store is not the shop window; it is making sure a double tap, a dropped connection or a late supplier answer never costs anyone money.

01

Idempotency keys

The pay button, kasir checkout and stock movements send an idempotency key; a repeat returns the first result. Each order reaches the supplier exactly once with its own reference, and an unclear result is only ever checked, never re-sent.

02

Bill-token binding

Checking a bill returns a token bound to that product and customer number, for that day. Paying a different number with it is refused; a bill counts as paid only with the supplier's serial number.

03

Locked shift close

Closing a kasir shift is atomic and locks the drawer. A sale or refund that arrives after the cash was counted is refused or flagged as a refund owed, so the drawer always matches.

04

Audit hash chain

hash = sha256(prev_hash . row) under a single locked head row, with a unique index on prev_hash against forks. A verifier re-reads the chain; a nightly anchor sends the latest hash to Telegram.

05

Held, not failed

When a refusal is on the store's side, the order is held and retried instead of failed. A late success on an order already failed is never applied automatically: it goes to the owner for review.

06

Permissions on the server

Per-person permissions are checked by the router and again by every API route. Cost and profit are not in the response at all for staff without the reports permission.

Architecture

One SPA, one API, two queues

Paid orders never wait behind slow jobs: orders run on their own queue, while Telegram, email, Sheets and PDF rendering run on another. The supplier sits behind a single gateway that owns the mode, the call budget, a circuit breaker and the hold-and-retry rules.
